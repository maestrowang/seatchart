01

Your first chart

Everything lives in the panel on the left. Work down it in order and you'll have a finished chart in a few minutes.

  1. Under Build Rows, set how many chairs you want, pick Curved or Straight, and click + Add Row. Repeat for each row.
  2. Click any chair to select it, then choose its instrument. The chair takes on that section's color.
  3. Open Roster at the top right and paste your musician names in.
  4. Use Print Chart or Download PDF when you're happy with it.

How the panel is organized

Click any section title to fold it away. Collapse All and Expand All sit at the bottom.

02

Building rows

Each row you add sits behind the last. Curved rows are shaped so their end chairs line up with the rows in front — the usual look for orchestral seating.

  • Undo Row removes the row you added most recently.
  • Reset Seating Chart clears everything and starts fresh.

When a row won't go in

The stage is a fixed size, so there's a real limit to how much fits. If a new row would overlap the chairs already there or run off the edge, the app tells you instead of adding a squashed or half-hidden row.

To make room, try any of these, then add the row again:

  • Lower Seat spacing to narrow the rows.
  • Lower Row spacing to close the gaps between rows.
  • Move Stage spacing to shift everyone forward.
Worth knowing

Past a certain point the stage really is full, and no amount of adjusting will fit another row.

Editing seats

The Selection panel changes to match whatever you've clicked, and opens on its own when you select something.

Add or remove oneShift + click
Move a chairDrag it anywhere you like

Rename on the chartDouble-click the chair

One chair selected

Set its instrument, its label, and its color. You can give it a music stand, hide it (handy for a partial row), or delete it. Reset turns it back into a plain unassigned chair.

Several chairs selected

The panel switches to bulk controls — assign one instrument to all of them, add stands, hide or show them, or delete them together. You can also straighten a run of chairs inside a curved row, which is useful for a piano, percussion, or a soloist out front.

The podium

Click the podium to rename it, change its color, or give it a music stand or conductor's stand. Disable Podium hides it completely.

04

Spacing & size

Four sliders sit under Tools. What they affect depends on whether anything is selected.

Nothing selectedChairs selected
Changes the whole chartChanges only what you've selected
SliderWhat it does
Seat sizeHow big the chairs are drawn.
Seat spacingHow far apart chairs sit within a row. On a curved row they spread along the curve, so the ends open up as much as the middle.
Row spacingHow far apart the rows sit from each other.
Stage spacingSlides everyone toward or away from the audience.

Everything moves from where it currently sits — including chairs you've dragged by hand and loose chairs that don't belong to a row. They travel with the rows around them rather than being left behind.

If a slider stops

The sliders stop when the chart reaches the edge of the visible area, or when chairs would start to overlap. Drag further and the handle springs back to the furthest setting that actually worked.

05

Extra tools

Three tools sit above the sliders. Press Esc to leave any of them.

Freeform Chair

Click anywhere to drop a chair that belongs to no row — good for a soloist, a piano bench, or anything off to one side.

Link Stand Partners

Click two chairs to have them share a music stand. Click the same pair again to unlink them. If you later delete one of a pair, the other keeps a stand of its own.

Text Box

Click anywhere to place a note on the chart. Double-click it to edit right there, and press Enter for a new line. Drag it to move it.

06

Musician names

The Roster button sits at the top right. Names attach to chairs in order — first name to the first chair, and so on down the section.

  1. Open the roster and choose a section from the dropdown.
  2. Click Paste / Edit Names and paste your list, one name per line. Pasting a column straight out of a spreadsheet works.
  3. Names appear on the chart straight away.

Ordering follows the usual convention: curved rows fill from the outside in, straight rows from the center out.

You can change that per section under View → Show seat rank numbers:

StandardFills row by row, front to back — the default.
Outer seat priorityFills the outer chairs of every row first, then comes back for the inner ones. Useful when the strongest musicians sit on the outside of each stand.
Invert rankingReverses whichever order you've chosen, so the last chair becomes first.
Hide rank numbersKeeps the ordering but takes the badges off the chart for that section.

Changing any of these re-seats that section's names straight away, so you can try one and see the result immediately.

Also worth trying

  • Drag names in the list to reorder them.
  • Double-click a name on the chart to fix a typo in place.
  • Shuffle reassigns a section at random. Tick Preserve stand partners to keep stand-sharing pairs together.
  • Names past the number of chairs available are flagged as unassigned.
  • Roster text size scales the names up or down.
Shuffling is safe

Shuffling never touches your real roster order. Revert to Original always brings it back, and you can shuffle over and over without reverting first.

Charts without instruments

You don't need to assign instruments to use names. If your chairs have no instrument, the roster groups them by row instead — Row 1, Row 2, and so on — filled left to right.

07

Groups

Groups lists every instrument section on your chart with a chair count, so you can select a whole section in one click.

You can also select any set of chairs and use Save Selection as Group… to name it and keep it for later — useful for something you re-select often, like "front stands" or "everyone on risers".

09

Saving & sharing

There are two ways to keep a chart, and the difference matters.

Saved charts

These live inside this browser on this computer. They're quick, but they won't follow you to another machine — and clearing your browsing data can remove them.

Chart files

Download Chart File saves your chart as a file on your computer. Upload Chart File… opens one back up.

Use chart files to move work between computers, send a chart to a colleague, or keep a backup you're confident in.

Recommended

For anything you'd be sorry to lose, download a chart file. It's the only copy that exists outside this browser.

Templates

Templates starts you from a ready-made chart. Loading one only affects your own copy — change it as freely as you like. Click Refresh List to check for new ones.

10

Printing & export

Three ways to get your chart off the screen.

Download PNGAn image file, with a quality choice that shows the file size before you commit.
Download PDFA proper PDF, same quality choices.
Print ChartPrints at the highest quality — no options to pick.

Color modes

PNG and PDF each offer three:

  • Digital — Full Color — exactly as it looks on screen.
  • Print — Color — white background, no stage texture. Kinder to paper and ink.
  • Print — Black & White — greyscale, adjusted so names and labels stay readable without their colors.
Tip

Ctrl+P (or ⌘+P) opens the app's own print dialog rather than your browser's, so you get just the chart instead of the whole page with the side panel.

12

Troubleshoot

A slider stops before I want it to

The chart has reached the edge of the visible area, or chairs would begin to overlap. The handle springing back is the app showing you the furthest setting that actually worked. Make room elsewhere first — often lowering Seat spacing frees up the most.

It won't let me add another row

There isn't room for one without overlapping or running off the stage. Lower the row and seat spacing, or move the formation forward with stage spacing, then try again. Sometimes the stage is genuinely full.

I moved a chair and now it won't line up

Select it and use Reset, or press Ctrl/⌘+Z to step back. Chairs you've moved by hand keep their position on purpose, so the sliders move them along with the row rather than snapping them back.

My names are on the wrong chairs

Names fill in rank order, so it usually means the list order needs adjusting rather than the chart. Drag names in the roster list, or double-click a name on the chart to correct it directly.

My saved charts have disappeared

Saved charts live in this browser. Clearing browsing data, switching browsers, or using a different computer will lose them. Download a chart file for anything you want to keep.

The black & white print is hard to read

Use Print — Black & White rather than printing the full-color version on a mono printer. It's adjusted so labels and names stay legible once the colors are gone.
